Guiana is not empty.

In 1596 Sir Walter Raleigh published a pamphlet asking England to take Guiana. His pamphlet says the land is rich, easy to take and untouched. His own pages show it is full of people, and that he plans to rule them. We ask you not to listen.

People Raleigh names

The pamphlet is the only source here, and we hear these people through Raleigh and his interpreters. Even so, they are in every chapter.

Topiawari

A very old chief, described as wise. He sighs over the loss of his country and liberty when he talks of conquest from the mountains (p. 107). Raleigh later takes his son to England (p. 111).

Morequito

A chief put to death by the Spanish commander Berrio (pp. 105, 109). His uncle Topiawari becomes Raleigh’s chief ally.

The Tivitivas

Houses on the ground in summer, in the trees in winter, because the river floods. Raleigh calls them handsome and brave (pp. 94–95).

The Arwacas, Nepoios and Orenoqueponi

Pilots, traders and hosts. Raleigh could not have travelled without them (pp. 93, 102).

What his plan would cost

For people

  • Raleigh asks the Queen to “hold it as tributary,” meaning the people would pay her, or to conquer and keep it (p. 123).
  • He says soldiers will “fight for gold” and pay themselves with plates of it (p. 119).
  • His own men burned the Spanish town of San Joseph and killed its guard (p. 83).

For land and rivers

  • He praises Guiana because its earth is “not torn,” its mines not broken and its temples not pulled down (p. 120). Then he proposes forts, mines and a soil for sugar and ginger (pp. 120–121).
  • He discusses digging up graves for treasure, and says doing it early would have lost him his allies (p. 118).

For truth

  • The shining stones he shows as proof turned out to be coloured stones with no gold in them (p. 110).
  • He insists on headless men, then says it matters little whether they are real (p. 111).

What you can do

  • Do not invest in the voyage. He promises a London “Contractation house” richer than Seville’s (p. 122).
  • Ask who gains. Raleigh’s own list is the Queen, the soldiers and the captains he would make kings (p. 123).
  • Read the pamphlet and look for the people he names.
